Process–Structure–Property Relationships in Thin-Film Electrodes

WO3 · Spray pyrolysis · Raman spectroscopy · XRD · SEM · Electrochemistry

This research investigates how fabrication conditions control the structure and electrochemical behavior of WO3 thin-film electrodes, with a particular focus on connecting processing, microstructure, and proton-storage response.

Overview of WO3 thin-film fabrication by spray pyrolysis, structural and morphological characterization, and electrochemical analysis.

Overview

WO3 is a promising electrode material for proton-based electrochemical energy storage because its structure can accommodate reversible proton insertion. However, its electrochemical behavior is strongly affected by film morphology, thickness, crystallinity, and processing conditions.

In this work, I investigated how spray-pyrolysis parameters modify WO3 thin-film structure and how those structural differences influence proton intercalation and charge-storage behavior.

Methods

Thin-Film Fabrication

WO3 thin films were prepared by spray pyrolysis under controlled synthesis conditions to systematically vary film morphology and structure.

Structural Characterization

Raman spectroscopy and X-ray diffraction were used to evaluate structural evolution, phase characteristics, and changes induced by processing conditions.

Morphology

Surface and cross-sectional SEM were used to examine film uniformity, roughness, porosity, and thickness-dependent microstructural changes.

Electrochemistry

Electrochemical measurements were used to evaluate proton insertion behavior and relate charge-storage characteristics to the underlying thin-film structure.

Key Findings

01

Processing controls microstructure

Changes in spray-pyrolysis conditions produced measurable differences in film morphology, thickness, and structural characteristics.

02

Structure governs electrochemical response

Proton-storage behavior varied systematically with the resulting thin-film structure, demonstrating a direct processing–structure–property relationship.

03

Film morphology affects charge-storage mechanism

Differences in film morphology and accessible surface area influenced the balance between diffusion-controlled and surface-dominated electrochemical processes.

Research Significance

This study demonstrates that electrochemical performance cannot be separated from the processing history and resulting microstructure of the electrode.

By connecting fabrication parameters with structural characterization and electrochemical response, this work provides a framework for rationally designing thin-film electrodes for proton-based energy storage systems.
