Process-Structure-Property Relationships in Thin-Film Electrodes

This study investigates how spray-pyrolysis processing conditions govern the microstructure and proton-storage behavior of WO3 thin-film electrodes. Substrate temperature and spray volume were systematically varied during deposition, producing films with thicknesses ranging from approximately 0.9 to 5.2 μm. The resulting films were characterized by SEM, Raman spectroscopy, and XRD, while cyclic voltammetry and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy were used to evaluate proton intercalation/deintercalation and charge-transfer behavior.

Changes in processing conditions produced pronounced differences in both film structure and electrochemical response. Proton intercalation varied from approximately 85% to 69%, while the charge-transfer resistance during proton insertion increased from approximately 1.9 Ω to 56.9 Ω depending on the deposition condition. By correlating these electrochemical responses with film morphology and structure, deposition conditions promoting reversible proton intercalation/deintercalation and stable electrode performance were identified.
